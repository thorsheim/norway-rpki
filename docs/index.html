Route-origin validity of routed ASNs

Each announcement seen by RIPE RIS was validated against the full global ROA set. All valid: every route validates. Partly valid: some routes are NotFound or Invalid. None valid: nothing protected.

Where ROAs are published

ROA prefixes naming a Norwegian ASN, by the repository that publishes them. A ROA is published by the certificate authority (CA) of whoever holds the address space, which is not always the network using it (provider-assigned or leased space). With a delegated CA the holder runs the CA itself; if it or its publication server stops working, its ROAs expire and routes lose protection. One ASN can appear under several repositories. Repository counts come from the console’s per-ASN pages, which can lag the validated data by a few hours.

Where the data comes from

  • Which ASNs are Norwegian: ASNs delegated to Norway by RIPE NCC (RIPEstat country resource list), plus ASNs whose registry country is NO in the ipverse asn-info dataset.
  • What each AS announces: exact prefixes from RIPEstat announced-prefixes (RIPE RIS), counting only routes seen by at least 10 RIS peers during the last day.
  • ROAs and ASPAs: the complete validated output (rpki.json) of the rpki-client console, covering all five RIR trust anchors. The time of the build is in Sources.
  • Upstream-side neighbours: RIPEstat asn-neighbours (left neighbours in RIS AS paths), with the number of RIS peers that see each link.
  • Where ROAs are published: the publication point (SIA) of every ROA and ASPA object on each ASN's rpki-client console page. Hosts are grouped as RIR/NIR-hosted CAs (e.g. rpki.ripe.net), delegated CAs that publish through an RIR service (RIPE NCC Publication as a Service, ARIN Repository Publication Service), and delegated CAs with their own or a third-party publication server.

How the checks work

  • Route validity: each announcement is validated per RFC 6811 against every VRP worldwide: Valid, Invalid (wrong origin AS, or longer than maxLength) or NotFound.
  • maxLength: a ROA is flagged when maxLength is longer than the prefix and more-specifics in that range are not all announced. “Wide” is IPv4 4+ bits, “loose” 1–3 bits; IPv6 from 16 bits.
  • Unannounced ROAs: ROA prefixes with no announcement from that AS inside or around them. When another Norwegian AS announces the space, it is flagged as a likely migration leftover.
  • Routed or all ASNs: by default every figure, table, finding, recommendation and change covers only ASNs that currently announce at least one prefix seen by 10 or more RIS peers. The switch at the top includes the unrouted ASNs too; your choice is remembered in this browser.
  • Comparing with Cloudflare Radar: Radar's “ASes” figure for a country adds the ASes announcing IPv4 to those announcing IPv6, so an AS that announces both is counted twice. Count the routed ASNs here the same way (IPv4 plus IPv6) to compare.
  • Changes: every update is compared with the previous one. Badges show the difference (+/−; ▲/▼ for route validity), coloured green when things improved and red when they got worse; hover, tap or focus a badge to see exactly what changed. The Changes tab keeps a log of every change for the last 30 days, with a chart per day.
  • ASPA: each provider set is compared with the neighbours RIS sees on the upstream side. RIS cannot tell peers from transit providers, so unlisted neighbours are something to check, not proven errors.

What this does not show

  • Routes seen by fewer than 10 RIS peers, or only inside Norway.
  • Whether Norwegian networks drop invalid routes (ROV enforcement).
  • AS0 ROAs and ROA expiry dates.
  • Norwegian networks that use an ASN registered in another country.
